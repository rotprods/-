## 0. Epistemic legend

- **CANON** — directly supported by project bible/data.
- **OBSERVED** — measured in accepted Blender r13 or current repo artifacts.
- **INFERRED** — consequence of canon that is useful but not itself stated canon.

- **BLOCKED** — cannot be fixed safely yet.

# 1. Visual North Star

> **SYLVA PRIME must look like a civilization negotiating occupancy inside a living information network, not like buildings placed in an alien forest.**

The world is recognized through **load, growth, memory and consent**.

Every important shape must answer at least one physical question:

1. What carries load?
2. What is growing?
3. What is transmitting information?
4. What was grafted by humans?
5. What is repaired, rejected, infected or voluntarily integrated?
6. What can the player traverse, read or disturb?

The world fails art direction if its identity depends on:
- purple lighting;
- glowing mushrooms;
- arbitrary vines;
- generic sci-fi plates;
- unreadable organic noise;
- text/logo overlays.

If all emissive signals and labels are removed, a Sylva asset must still read as **grown load-path + information ecology + negotiated human intervention**.

# 3. Primary art pillars

## PILLAR A — STRUCTURE IS BIOLOGY

**Rule:** roots are not decoration; they are beams, bridges, arteries and message paths.

A root's thickness, branching and direction must imply:
- load;
- nutrient flow;
- travel;
- signaling;
- repair history.

Do not add a root because an empty space “needs detail”.

## PILLAR B — INFORMATION HAS PHYSICAL TOPOLOGY

Signals must appear to move through a network with place and latency.

Use redundant carriers:
- geometry repetition;
- membrane tension/pulse;
- directional growth ridges;
- mechanical/organic sound;
- color only as one layer.

Never make “cyan glow” the sole meaning of memory or “red glow” the sole danger telegraph.

## PILLAR C — HUMAN OCCUPATION IS A GRAFT

Human construction is not a separate architectural layer floating above nature.

It must:
- clamp;
- suspend;
- bridge;
- brace;
- drain;
- inspect;
- repair;
- route utilities around living tissue.

The player should see where a human component contacts the host and imagine how maintenance happens.

## PILLAR D — CONSENT / CONFLICT LEAVES MATERIAL EVIDENCE

**INFERRED from narrative:** integration is socially contested.

Art should distinguish:
- cooperative graft — broad interfaces, accommodated growth, maintained clamps;
- forced occupation — compressed tissue, cut growth, emergency braces, scars;
- abandonment — host overgrowth reclaiming hardware;
- repair — patched membrane, replaced brackets, new growth around old wounds.

Do not encode morality as “good = clean / bad = dirty”. Show cause.

## PILLAR E — READABILITY SURVIVES ORGANIC COMPLEXITY

Organic forms never get permission to destroy gameplay readability.

Primary route hierarchy must remain visible via:
- clear openings;
- repeated structural rhythm;
- stable silhouette;
- negative space;
- physically distinct boundary forms;
- redundant signal grammar.

# 4. Shape language

## 4.1 Living macrostructure — PROPOSAL

Dominant primitives:
- tapered cylinders with nonuniform elliptical compression;
- Y / unequal bifurcation;
- compression arches;
- buttresses;
- saddle junctions;
- tension membranes;
- callus terraces;
- fibrous ribbons following load direction.

Avoid:
- perfect tentacles;
- spaghetti tangles;
- spherical “alien pods” without attachment logic;
- fractal branching at every scale;
- arbitrary spikes.

### Branch hierarchy

Visual rule:

`primary trunk/root > secondary load path > local connector > membrane/callus interface > microfiber`

Child mass should not visually exceed parent mass unless another support path is obvious.

### Current macro scale reference — OBSERVED / PROPOSAL

Accepted r13 primary-root proxy radii:
- R01: 48 m
- R02: 62 m
- R03: 72 m
- R04: 36 m
- R05: 30 m
- R06: 40 m

These values establish macro silhouette only; they are not a reusable meso-module standard.

## 4.2 Human intervention — PROPOSAL

Counter-language:
- orthogonal or triangulated frames;
- explicit clamp rings;
- short repeatable spans;
- visible inspection access;
- deliberate dimensional regularity.

Humans should look **precise but dependent**; the host looks irregular but structurally coherent.

Never float a hard-surface panel on bark. Every plate needs:
- contact;
- clamp/fastener/strap;
- load path;
- maintenance reason.

## 4.3 Membranes — PROPOSAL

Membrane geometry must show:
- anchor points;
- direction of tension;
- sag where unloaded;
- reinforced edge where stressed;
- patch or scar where repaired.

Avoid perfect cloth-noise displacement over a flat plane.

# 7. Material anatomy

## 7.1 Mature structural root

### Macro
- taper;
- load-bearing asymmetry;
- flattening at compression/contact;
- bifurcation based on support.

### Meso
- fiber bundles;
- bark/callus transition;
- local ridges following stress;
- graft scars;
- protected signaling grooves where supported by design.

### Micro
- pores/fibers;
- moisture variation;
- small fissures along stress direction;
- biological residue in recesses.

**Anti-CGI:** no isotropic noise over the whole surface.

## 7.2 Young growth

Younger tissue should read through:
- smoother fiber organization;
- different stiffness implied by curvature;
- fewer old scars;
- fresher interfaces with mature root.

Do not merely make mature bark greener.

## 7.3 Traversable callus

Functionally analogous to a biological wear surface:
- compressed crown;
- polished contact bands;
- edge thickening;
- drainage/runoff where appropriate;
- repair growth after repeated traffic.

## 7.4 Membrane

Surface channels:
- body translucency/subsurface only if runtime budget allows;
- tension lines from anchors;
- local thickening near attachment;
- patches/scars;
- condensate only where physically motivated.

## 7.5 Soil / substrate

Do not use generic forest mud.

Substrate should show relation to host network:
- organic/mineral mix;
- root-induced compaction;
- depressions/channeling near structural masses;
- player/fauna traffic where relevant;
- local deposition.

R6 contains two catchment/depression hypotheses; these remain production shaping, not canon waterways.

## 7.6 Graft metal / human hardware

Manufacturing candidates:
- formed sheet;
- machined clamp surfaces;
- bolted/removable joints;
- replaceable straps;
- ceramic/insulating separators where function requires.

Wear:
- clamp pressure marks;
- oxide/moisture streaks;
- tool handling;
- grease only at mechanisms;
- field replacement mismatches.

Never distribute edge wear uniformly.

# 8. Growth and manufacturing causality

## 8.1 Living construction questions

Before approving a biological asset:
- Where did growth start?
- What constrained it?
- What load is it carrying?
- Why did it branch here?
- Which side sees compression/tension?
- How does it heal?
- What happens if it is cut?
- How does water/material drain around it?

## 8.2 Human construction questions

Before approving graft architecture:
- How was it installed without destroying the host?
- Can a worker inspect/remove it?
- How does differential growth/movement avoid tearing the connection?
- Where does runoff go?
- How are utilities routed?
- What is disposable vs structural?

# 9. Wear / history contract

## Living surfaces

Allowed causal wear:
- compression polish;
- abrasion from player/fauna traffic;
- tearing at excessive tension;
- callus after injury;
- old graft scar;
- sap/resin residue;
- localized biological colonization.

Forbidden:
- random scratch map;
- all edges equally worn;
- dirt gradient independent of gravity/contact.

## Human surfaces

Use:
- hand/tool contact;
- clamp adjustment marks;
- recent weld/repair vs older oxidation;
- moisture runoff;
- dust/deposition in sheltered cavities.

# 11. Biome composition

## Macro density

Use three simultaneous scales:

### Primary
- terrain relief;
- structural roots;
- city/region silhouettes;
- major negative space.

### Secondary
- route branches;
- membranes;
- buttresses;
- architecture clusters;
- large vegetation masses.

### Tertiary
- foliage;
- scar tissue;
- hardware;
- small organisms;
- decals/residue.

**Rule:** tertiary density is prohibited from compensating for weak primary composition.

## Distribution logic

Vegetation/fauna/scatter teams must use ecological rules, not decorative random scatter:
- proximity to nutrient/water/light opportunities once those systems are canonically defined;
- disturbance zones;
- stable-path avoidance or attraction by species;
- human occupation pressure;
- root-network relationships.

- motivated sources only;
- preserve floor/enemy/exit readability;

# 21. Anti-CGI / anti-generic checklist

Reject an asset if any are true:

- detail exists without function;
- roughness noise is uniformly distributed;
- every edge is worn;
- roots have constant radius;
- branches ignore load;
- membranes ignore anchors;
- human hardware floats;
- all plants emit light;
- glow substitutes for navigation;
- scale cannot be inferred;
- architecture has facade but no structure/utilities/access;
- creature anatomy is only a mashup of familiar animals;
- vehicle mechanism is unexplained;
- damage ignores direction/force/use;
- “alien” identity relies on purple/teal color grading;
- kitbash parts imply incompatible manufacturing.
